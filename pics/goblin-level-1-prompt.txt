Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: single enemy sprite for Axe & Sword, a text RPG in a minimalist dark interface.
Create one ordinary level-one goblin, full body, small skinny body, disproportionately large head, long pointed ears, dirty olive skin, tiny red eyes the only saturated accent. Hunched ready-to-attack stance, feet apart. A plain short dull iron sword clearly projecting out from one hand, a small round old wooden shield in the other, dirty leather shoulder strap and simple worn brown loincloth. No extra gear. Dangerous but clearly a common weak enemy.
Authentic low-resolution 16-bit pixel art, NOT a detailed painting with a pixelation filter. Design on a strict 128x128 logical pixel canvas and show it enlarged with nearest-neighbor only, every logical pixel the same square size, all contours snapping to that grid. Goblin including weapon occupies approximately 65x75 logical pixels, centered with abundant empty dark space. Bold economical clusters, very low detail, prioritizing goblin then sword then shield then combat pose. At most 16 muted flat colors, olive greens, dark browns, ochre, desaturated blue-grey iron, tiny bone-colored accents and tiny red eyes. Deep shadows, broken selective contours that disappear in shadows, never uniform black outlines. Light from upper left. No smooth shading, no antialiasing, no blur, no subpixel details, no fine square brushwork or noisy dithering.
Background completely uniform near-black green-grey #101412, no visible card border, no frame or interface. Beneath feet only a tiny vignette of muddy brown earth and a few stones; dense central earth breaks into smaller separated pixel clusters then a few lone square pixels then empty background. No gradient or transparency fade. No landscape, no horizon, no text, no logo, no glow. Square composition. The result must read instantly at 128x128.

Production: built-in image_gen. Generated at larger resolution, then sampled with nearest-neighbor at 128x128 and mapped to a fixed 16-color palette without dithering. Preview is exact 6x pixel replication. This is a normalized low-resolution export, not a natively generated 128x128 source.
